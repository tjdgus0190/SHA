Problems that keep coming back when you color

Whether you've been drawing for ten years or ten days, color tends to trip everyone up in the same places.

Every color change throws your values off and flattens the form

Rotate the hue in a standard color picker and the lightness shifts along with it. Matching by eye, your shadow and highlight values slowly drift apart, and the finished piece loses depth. SHA Paint locks your current lightness and moves only hue and saturation, so your values have nothing to drift from.

Finding a color that fits your current one takes forever

Want some violet in a shadow or a touch of yellow in the light? In a normal picker you rotate the hue, then fix the lightness, then repeat. SHA Paint's field only shows colors at the same value as your current color, so just drag around it and you get colors that fit, ready to use.

You leave the drawing just to pick a color

That quick trip up to the color panel breaks your focus more than you'd expect. The palette opens right by your cursor, so you can change colors without leaving your current view or zoom level.

You keep hunting for colors you already chose

Skin tones and character colors get reused dozens of times in a single piece. Instead of sampling them again with the eyedropper, your 8 most recent colors stay as swatches, one click away.

FAQ

Does it work on Mac?

It's Windows-only for now.

Is the app in English?

The palette popup is almost entirely visual, but the installer, menus, and settings are currently in Korean. The FAQ entries below walk you through the screens you'll need.

Do I need to keep a panel open in Photoshop?

We recommend opening the SHA Paint panel once from Photoshop's Plugins menu and docking it in a corner. Color detection while drawing with a pen is most reliable when the panel is connected. If a color fails to apply, a notice appears at the top of the popup.

What about Clip Studio Paint or other apps?

They aren't supported right now. Most apps other than Photoshop don't offer an official way for outside programs to set colors, so we won't claim that it works.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC" when I run it

That's expected. SHA Paint is a new app that isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen shows its standard warning for programs it hasn't seen before. Click "More info", then "Run anyway", to install. It isn't a virus or malware.

Where do I find my license key?

It's in the purchase confirmation email you get after checkout. Paste it into the license key screen during installation (the screen with a single text box).
